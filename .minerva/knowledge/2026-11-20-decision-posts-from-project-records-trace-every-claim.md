# Posts built from project records trace every factual claim to a source line

**Date**: 2026-11-20
**Type**: decision
**Theme**: blog-posts
**Summary**: Record-based posts cite every fact; first-person narration may not assert unrecorded facts or verdicts.
**Context**: .minerva/work/2026-11-20-seekless-ad-postmortem-post (see git history if the worktree has been cleaned up)

## Context
Two posts were drafted from the owner's other repositories: a postmortem of Seekless's October 2026 ad campaigns and an explainer on SoberJourney's AI Sponsor safety design. The owner's standing rule is no invented copy. Each draft went through several verification passes, and each pass removed another layer of framing, because the success criterion said figures must trace to sources but never said where the author's own narration ends and an unsupported claim begins.

## Finding
The criterion that settled it: every factual claim — figures, events, decisions, who did what, what a platform or tool can do — traces to a cited source line listed in the work unit's scratchpad, with computed figures shown with their inputs. The author's first-person narration (headings, transitions, framing) is allowed only where it asserts no fact, experience, ranking, inference or verdict the records do not contain. The owner of the source repository is the post's author, so first-person narration of the owner's recorded actions and stated preferences is sourced.

## Implications
- Write the criterion this way from the start of any record-based post; a looser wording costs repeated verify-and-replan cycles.
- The common failures were absolutes ("every change", "four weeks" of ads when four were only planned), lessons the records do not draw, typicality claims ("the usual friction"), and actions attributed to the author that the records leave unattributed.
- Opinions the owner wants in a post have to come from the owner; they cannot be supplied by the drafter.
