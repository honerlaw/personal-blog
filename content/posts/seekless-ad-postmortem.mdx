[Seekless](https://www.seekless.ai) is a job search tool I have been building this year. It reads company career pages directly, lets you search them in plain language, and has a few tools on top: a free résumé checker, résumé tailoring for a specific job, and interview practice.

Seekless came out of interviews with friends and coworkers about their job searches. They were looking for specific jobs and could not find them, so I built a crawler that queries company websites directly. They were using Claude or ChatGPT to tailor each résumé, so I built tailoring into the same system. They wanted a way to track their applications, and when they reached the interview stage, they wanted to practice in a setting closer to the real thing. Those features came from the problems they described and what they were doing about them.

In October I ran a paid video campaign to get people to remember it. I planned four weeks and $1,500. I stopped the ads after about two weeks, having spent $614.75 on short video ads across YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram and Reddit. Those ads were shown 221,642 times and clicked 1,322 times. They produced 3 engaged visits, and no sign-ups came from sessions that started on an ad.

These are the real numbers, what I think went wrong, and what I would do differently.

## The bet

This was not my first paid campaign. Earlier Reddit ads (in July and October) had spent $636.92, and ChatGPT ads (from September 26 to October 10) had spent $427.52. Both were set up to buy clicks, and neither produced a sign-up I could see. That evidence was weak, because sign-ups were not being recorded for much of that time. But the pattern was clear enough: weekly visitors rose from between 40 and 60 to about 230 while the ads ran, and fell straight back when they stopped.

So I changed the goal. Someone scrolling a feed is not looking for a job at that moment. Their intent is close to zero. Instead of trying to make them click, I wanted Seekless to stick with them, so that they would come back on their own later, when they were actually searching. On TikTok, Instagram and Reddit, the ads were set up to buy reach and views, not clicks. YouTube could only bid on clicks through the API, so it ran on a low target cost per click: impressions people scroll past are free, so a low bid buys cheap exposure.

The success measure followed from that. When I planned it, I was getting about 3 to 4 sign-ups a week that did not come from an ad, and the target was 8 a week by the end of the four weeks.

The plan:

- $1,500 over four weeks, with an early read at week two
- $60 a day across four platforms: TikTok $20, YouTube Shorts $15, Instagram $15 and Reddit $10
- two videos of about 22 seconds each, run everywhere, so the campaign would also test which opening worked better

Video A opened with a pile: job boards, résumé files and a tracking spreadsheet stacking up, then the line "one place for the whole search". Video B opened with a question: "Still sending the same résumé to every job?" I came to like B best.

## What happened

The campaign went live on the evening of October 19.

B beat A on every downstream measure where the two were compared. On YouTube, B drew 334 clicks in the first week to A's 133. Meta put most of the Instagram budget behind B. TikTok bought completed views more cheaply than anything else, at about $0.04 each.

None of that turned into use. After the first week the four platforms had spent $416.82 for 163,758 impressions, and the result was 2 engaged visits and 0 sign-ups. That is about $208 for each visit where someone actually did something on the site.

I was extremely disappointed. I really thought the campaign would get more engagement than that. It did make the next step easier, though: it pushed me to pivot and figure out what would actually get people to engage with the app.

Meanwhile I had started a small Google Search campaign on October 22, aimed at people looking for job sites or a résumé tool. From October 22 to 26 it spent $89.56 and produced 27 finished résumé checks, about $3.32 each. I had also restarted the ChatGPT ads on October 22, at $25 a day outside the video budget.

On October 26 I stopped TikTok, Instagram, Reddit and ChatGPT. By then ChatGPT had spent $96.51 for 40 clicks and no engaged visits. I kept YouTube running with video B only, moved the main focus to Search, and made two more YouTube videos built on what B did well: C pointed at the résumé checker, and D at résumé tailoring.

They did not help. From October 26 to November 2, YouTube spent $199.75 and brought 506 sessions from its ads. One of those became an engaged visit. None became a sign-up. Video D hit the $40 rule, $40 of spend with no outcome, and was paused. Then I looked at YouTube as a whole: about $200 per engaged visit, against about $5.06 per finished résumé check on Search from October 27 to November 2. I stopped YouTube that evening and moved its $25 a day to Search, two weeks before its close-out rule was due.

The final numbers for the video campaign:

- $614.75 spent of the $1,500 budget
- 221,642 impressions and 1,322 clicks
- YouTube bought the cheapest clicks, at about $0.26 each
- 3 engaged visits, 0 finished résumé checks and 0 sign-ups from sessions that started on an ad

## The measure I could not read

The success measure, sign-ups that did not come from an ad, did not move while the ads ran. It was 2 a week in both live weeks. The final count for the two weeks before launch was 3 and 6.

It also became unreadable. A scraper started hammering the site during the campaign, presenting itself as old versions of Chrome, Firefox and Edge. Sessions that did not come from a campaign went from 143 in the week of October 12 to 1,518 the next week and 12,120 the week after that. The before-and-after comparison the plan depended on could not be made from those counts.

Sign-ups did jump later: 44 in the week of November 2 and 198 the week after. That came after every feed ad had stopped. Most of those accounts carried a click id from an ad, and the rise lines up with other things: Search ads for interview practice, and posting the same videos as regular unpaid posts on November 2. Many of those sign-ups were also pranks (more on that below). None of it is credited to the video campaign.

## What Search showed

The biggest lesson for me was that Seekless depends on people who already intend to do something. Someone browsing a feed who sees a job search platform is very unlikely to engage, or to come back later. Someone searching for a job, for AI interview practice, for résumé tailoring or for a résumé check is exponentially more likely to use it.

When the résumé checker got its own Search campaign, about 43% of the measurable sessions from its ads in the first days (November 13 to 17) ended with a finished check.

Search was not a clean win either, though. A finished check is not a sign-up, and only 2 accounts came from those ads in the same days. Counting all of the first Search campaign's spend from October 26 to November 12, a finished check cost about $7.57.

Search ads for interview practice drew a wave of sign-ups on phones. Of the new accounts from November 11 to the morning of November 12, 34 started a practice call, and judging from their first answers, 22 of them pranked the AI interviewer.

I do not think that was funny so much as a lesson. We got a lot of new sign-ups over a few days, but actual engagement with the app stayed about the same. I was paying for people to joke around with the tool and leave. Sign-ups are not as important as engagement.

## Why the video campaign failed

Looking back, the campaign never really tested the idea it was built on.

- **Too thin to build memory.** $60 a day spread across four platforms meant each person saw an ad about once. An awareness campaign usually needs three or more views per person. I bought single views on four platforms instead of repeated views on one.
- **The ad and the page said different things.** On October 23, a few days in, the home page changed to lead with "Tailor your résumé. Practice the interview." The ads still carried the campaign message, "one place for the whole job search". Someone who clicked landed on a page making a different promise.
- **I could not see the result.** The measure depended on unpaid counts in the analytics tool, and the scraper flooded those counts.

## How it was run

One part of this did work.

An AI agent ran almost all of the campaign through the ad platforms' APIs. At launch, a safety check blocked some of the agent's switches; I approved them, and switched Instagram on myself in Ads Manager. The agent built every campaign paused, dry-ran each Google Ads change before making it, and read the state back after the changes it made. It kept a dated record of every decision with my reasoning attached, and a check-in log with the figures and where each one came from.

The plan had decision rules from the start. The two that ended up being applied, video D's $40 no-outcome rule and a close-out rule for YouTube, were agreed on October 26. The agent proposed stops either from those rules (video D, and YouTube's close-out rule applied two weeks early) or from the latest figures (the feed platforms). It showed the numbers and asked before pausing anything, and I made every call.

It also ran into friction. The Google Ads API cannot create YouTube video reach campaigns, so YouTube ran as a Demand Gen campaign, which the API does support. Meta would not take a card until the ad account had a country, and that had to be set through the API. ChatGPT ads would not accept a daily budget under $25.

I would definitely do it again. It made it quick to understand the campaigns that were running, adjust them and run experiments. Today I can stop a campaign and create a new one across several platforms in minutes, through Claude.

## What I would do next time

- **Pick one platform and buy repeated views.** Three or more views per person on one feed, instead of one view each on four.
- **Land on a page that says what the ad says.** If the message changes, the ads change with it.
- **Measure from the product, not the analytics tool.** Use attribution and product activity in my own database, not unpaid visitor counts.
- **Read the Search campaigns before crediting the feeds.** When sign-ups rise while several campaigns are running, check the others first.
- **Measure engagement, not sign-ups.** A wave of sign-ups that never use the product is money spent on nothing.

For now I have reduced the budget and kept only what engaged users the most: Google Search ads on the free résumé checker and interview practice. Those are the ads that brought people in and kept them coming back, because they reach people while they are already looking.
